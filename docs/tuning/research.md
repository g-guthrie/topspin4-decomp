# Retune Top Spin 4 around earned risk

> **Correction (2026-11-14):** the decompiled launch commands the *initial* (racket) speed in km/h, not the arrival speed (`docs/gameplay/gp4b.md` §0.2). The arrival-speed conversion discussed below is therefore unnecessary; see `docs/gameplay/shot-templates.md` for measured template values versus real tennis.


Top Spin 4 should be retuned around one rule: **risk is something the player chooses, its size depends on situations both players can see, and every strong option has a reachable counter**. Flat nerfs to peak shots are the wrong tool. The evidence lines up behind this. TS4's only gameplay-balance download went after serves and serve-boosting coach skills after players complained of "unreturnable serve after unreturnable serve". TopSpin 2K25's eventual fix for its flat-power meta left perfectly timed shots alone and instead raised error on *imperfect* power shots taken when tired or deep in a rally, while making the slice alternative cheaper. Real tennis gives hard targets. Errors end about two-thirds of points. Servers win about 69-75% of first-serve points and about 55% of second-serve points. Serve-and-volley wins 65-70% of the time, but only because players pick their moments. Drop shots win only about 54% for the hitter, and about one in five is an unforced error. TS4's decompiled engine can reach these targets through data, not code. Drag rate, spin drift and bounce restitution/friction/spin decay all live in shot-template rows. Launch speed is commanded as a km/h *arrival* speed. The reaction delay, reach-speed tables and timing-grade thresholds are config values. Two findings change the plan most. First, the flight model's drag is linear in velocity, while real drag is quadratic, so one drag constant cannot fit both serves and lobs and has to be set per template. Second, the first bounce snaps to the planned landing point, so shot error has to be applied to the aim point and the outcome mode *before* launch; it cannot emerge from the flight. The final section turns this into lever-by-lever targets. Every target is labelled as either a sourced real-world anchor or a proposal, and the section lists what must be measured from the game's data before any number is changed.

## Serves were TS4's fault line, and the community's evidence is thin

The record of TS4's competitive meta is sparse, and that should limit how confidently anyone talks about "what was broken". Most 2011 community sources (GameFAQs, Operation Sports, GameSpot) could not be read in full, and no Reddit material surfaced, so much of what follows rests on search snippets and single posters. One signal is clear, though. 2K shipped a separate **Player Balancing Update** that tuned **Power serves, Advanced (right-stick) serves, and the coach skills Serve Stick Berserker, Drop Shot Artist and Focus Service**, after online players complained of "unreturnable serve after unreturnable serve" ([OS forum](https://forums.operationsports.com/forums/tennis/477387-top-spin-4-player-balancing-update-available.html)). The full May 2011 title update had **no gameplay-balance changes**. It covered mod detection, lobby, NAT and trophy fixes ([Just Push Start](https://www.justpushstart.com/2011/05/top-spin-4-patch-released/)). The serve, together with skills that multiply the serve, is therefore the first tuning lever, and return difficulty should depend on the returner's Reflexes and Speed as well as the server's stats.

The developers' stated design was three balanced archetypes: serve-and-volley (Sampras), offensive baseliner (Serena Williams) and defensive baseliner (Murray) ([Game Informer dev diary](https://www.gameinformer.com/b/news/archive/2011/02/10/top-spin-dev-diary.aspx)). TS4 removed TS3's per-stat point buy. Each level-up goes into one of three style tracks, each track raises a fixed bundle of the eight attributes, and **total level caps at 20 across all tracks**. Coaches add flat bonuses of **gold +50, silver +35, bronze +25** and unlock skills ([GameFAQs](https://gamefaqs.gamespot.com/boards/605747-top-spin-4/58105647)). A fan superguide credits these batch upgrades with ending TS3's "all power, no volley/stamina" builds and making serve-and-volley viable again ([Superguide](http://www.topspinsuperguides.com/topspin4.htm)). Community folklore points to a sharp threshold: going to the net with **Volley under 50 reportedly loses about 75% of the time** ([GameFAQs](https://gamefaqs.gamespot.com/boards/605746-top-spin-4/58508655?page=1)), and one reviewer said that without high volley stats you must time volleys very precisely or give points away ([Indian Video Gamer](https://www.indianvideogamer.com/?p=5886)). That suggests stats widen the timing window or soften the error penalty, and that their effect is non-linear. Both are claims to test against the decompiled curves, not facts.

Critics praised TS4 as the deepest tennis sim of its era (Metacritic **84 on 360, 82 on PS3**; GameSpot 9/10) for its timing, positioning and measured pace. The two complaints that recur are **slow, awkward net play** (The Observer) and **timing feedback that feels "nebulous"** ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Top_Spin_4); [GamesRadar](https://www.gamesradar.com/top-spin-4-review-10)). The AI was said to punish sloppy approaches with "ruthless passing shots" ([Gameranx quoting Game Informer](https://gameranx.com/game_main/gameid/top-spin-4-xbox360/)). On drop shots the evidence is mixed. Drop Shot Artist was nerfed, yet one poster said clean drop-shot winners were already hard to land, and players report that even perfectly timed drops land deep unless the stick is held back ([GameFAQs](https://gamefaqs.gamespot.com/boards/605747-top-spin-4/58468949)). No source documents lob, angle or AI-pattern exploits. Treat those as unexamined, not absent. An unverified 2025 Steam claim of a "perfect timing every shot" glitch with auto positioning ([Steam](https://steamcommunity.com/app/1785650/discussions/0/4516632348042834638)) is worth checking in the timing-grade code, but nothing should rest on it. The core to preserve is clear: timing plus positioning plus pace. The pain points to fix are net-play feel, timing legibility, serve dominance, and drop shots whose result does not track the setup.

## TopSpin 2K25 fixed flat power with conditional error, not flat nerfs

TopSpin 2K25 ran the same experiment publicly and offers the best set of patch lessons. At launch, timing set shot power and Rally Energy was the main limiter ([Stevivor](https://stevivor.com/reviews/topspin-2k25-review-power-shots-and-slow-progression/)). "How to beat power players" guides became a genre of their own ([gameplay.tips](https://gameplay.tips/guides/topspin-2k25-how-to-deal-with-power-players.html)). 2K published no numbers and worked in stages. **Patch 1.10 (July 2024)** rebalanced serves. The Serve attribute counts for more. **Wide serves spread more than centre serves**. Spread past the sideline on power and advanced serves **turns into net errors**. The perfect window is slightly shorter. Servers can stand slightly less far from centre ([MP1st 1.10](https://mp1st.com/news/topspin-2k25-update-1-10-released-for-serve-balancing-and-more-this-july-15)). **Patch 1.17 (November)** standardised coach skills to one at Tier 3 and two at Tier 4, and previewed the power fix ([MP1st 1.17](https://mp1st.com/title-updates-and-patches/topspin-2k25-update-1-17-swats-out-for-gameplay-improvements)). The **December 9, 2024** patch then added **more errors on Flat Power shots hit with low Rally Energy and in long rallies when timing isn't perfect**, slightly more errors on other groundstrokes under the same conditions, and **faster energy drain when Flat Power is repeated within a rally**, alongside cheaper slice power ([2K Support, snippet](https://support.2k.com/hc/en-us/articles/36285067828115-TopSpin-2K25-Patch-Update-December-9-2024)).

The design has four parts, and all of them carry over to TS4. A perfect, fresh shot keeps its full ceiling, so mastery still pays. Error grows only through *legible state*: imperfect timing, fatigue and rally length. Spamming a shot carries a mounting resource cost. Every nerf comes with a viable alternative, in this case slice. The serve pass adds one more principle: **risk scales with how extreme the target is**, so the server has to decide something instead of owning a free ace lane. The patch sequence (attributes, then serve, then AI, then flat power and energy) also suggests that a single sweeping retune is too risky to ship at once.

Other tennis games show what failure looks like, in both directions. AO Tennis 2 was criticised for clean winners "from nowhere" and an **overpowered drop shot**, and also for movement that didn't match the animations ([Game Informer](https://www.gameinformer.com/review/ao-tennis-2/ao-tennis-2-review-too-many-unforced-errors)). Matchpoint went the other way and was "almost harder to miss" than AO Tennis ([Gamereactor](https://embed.gamereactor.eu/matchpoint-tennis-championships-1116273/)). Push Square noted that timing matters less there than in TS4 ([Push Square](https://www.pushsquare.com/reviews/ps5/matchpoint-tennis-championships)). Tennis World Tour 2 uses hold time as its risk dial, with an accuracy penalty for overcharging ([SelectButton](https://selectbutton.com/reviews/tennis-world-tour-2-review)). AO Tennis 2 ties precision to stamina ([Operation Sports](https://operationsports.com/ao-tennis-2-review-another-step-towards-the-grand-slam)). In Mario Tennis Aces, trick shots with too much reach and defensive outlier characters dominated ([The Outer Haven](https://theouterhaven.net/2018/07/mario-tennis-aces-review)). The common thread is that **players accept errors and winners when the setup explains them and reject them when they feel random**. The drop shot is the shot each of these series gets wrong: overpowered in AO Tennis 2, unreliable in 2K25 ([CGMagazine](https://www.cgmagonline.com/review/game/top-spin-2k25-ps5-review/)). It needs a positional success model and a guaranteed counter. No source anywhere published dispersion values, timing-window frames or energy costs for any tennis game, so every number in the lever section is a proposal.

## Real balls lose a third of their speed before bouncing

The physics anchors are well established for serves, groundstroke averages, spin and aerodynamics, and thin for volleys, drops and lobs. **ATP first serves average about 187 km/h and second serves about 151 km/h. WTA top-10 averages are about 164 and 136 km/h** ([Bruin Sports Analytics](https://www.bruinsportsanalytics.com/post/serve_success)). Australian Open Hawk-Eye medians are **179 km/h for men and 153 km/h for women** ([Kovalchik & Reid 2017](https://www.jssm.org/jssm-16-489.xml-Fulltext)). The 2025 season leader averaged 216 km/h on first serves ([ATP Tour](https://www.atptour.com/en/news/scorecards-mpetshi-perricard-first-serve-speed)), and the record is 263.4 km/h (not ATP-recognised) ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fastest_recorded_tennis_serves)). Elite flat and kick serves measured about **52 m/s and 41 m/s**, with spins of about **1,213 rpm (flat), 2,215 rpm (slice) and 3,209 rpm (kick)** ([Sakurai, Reid & Elliott 2013](https://lida.sport-iat.de/twm/Record/4028109?lng=en)). Top-10 men's forehands at AO 2021 averaged **126.5-132.7 km/h** ([Too Many Rackets](https://toomanyrackets.com/forehand-speeds-in-pro-tennis/)), and ATP backhand medians sit at **97-121 km/h** ([Stats on the T](https://on-the-t.github.io/2016/10/22/AOLeaderboard-Backhand-Speed)). Topspin runs from about **2,700 rpm (Federer average) to 3,200-3,300 rpm (Nadal average)**, with peaks around 4,000-4,500 rpm ([TennisPlayer](https://media.tennisplayer.net/members/mystheavyball/john_yandell/modern_heavy_ball)). A wind-tunnel study treats 2,750 rpm as a typical pro topspin shot ([Goodwill, Chin & Haake 2004](https://shura.shu.ac.uk/634)). Hawk-Eye forehand net clearance averages **0.6 m or less for about 20 ATP players, and 0.78 m for Nadal** ([Stats on the T](https://on-the-t.github.io/2016/11/05/AOLeaderboard-Line-Hitting)). No public figures were found for volley, smash, drop or lob speeds, or for landing-depth histograms.

Free-flight tests give **C_D ≈ 0.507 (SD 0.024) for new balls, with no significant dependence on speed or spin across 14-30 m/s**. Lift rises roughly linearly with spin parameter S = rω/v, giving **C_L ≈ 0.1-0.3 for S ≈ 0.14-0.53**. The reference constants are ρ = 1.21 kg/m³ and A = 0.0034 m², and at 30 m/s drag is about 1.7 times the ball's weight ([TWU aerodynamics study](https://twu.tennis-warehouse.com/learning_center/aerodynamics2.php)). This is where TS4's model departs from reality in a way that matters. The decompiled flight equation is `v' = -k(v - W) - g`: drag is *linear* in velocity, with a constant rate k = T/a4 (1/s) per template. Real drag is quadratic. Its deceleration is k_q·v², with k_q = ½ρC_DA/m ≈ **0.0197 m⁻¹** for C_D 0.55 and m 57.5 g. That makes the equivalent linear rate k_q·v, which depends on speed. *Derived from the cited constants:* it is about **1.0 s⁻¹ for a 52 m/s serve, 0.65 s⁻¹ for a 33 m/s rally drive and 0.3 s⁻¹ for a 15 m/s lob or drop**. A single global drag rate will make either lobs float or serves die. Because T is a template field, the fix is a drag rate per template, fitted to that template's typical speed.

The second consequence concerns speed units. TS4 commands shots by **arrival speed in km/h** (speed at the first bounce), while every published figure is a radar **impact** speed ([Kovalchik & Reid 2017](https://www.jssm.org/jssm-16-489.xml-Fulltext)). Using v(x) ≈ v₀·e^(−k_q·x), *derived, not measured*: a 187 km/h first serve travelling about 18.5 m to the bounce arrives at about **130 km/h**, a 151 km/h second serve at about 105 km/h, and a 125 km/h forehand travelling about 21 m at about **82 km/h**. These estimates ignore gravity and lift, which add a little speed on descent. They need checking against the game's own command values and against tracking data. The notes' integrations also put a 200 km/h serve at the bounce in about 0.40 s and at the returner in about 0.65-0.75 s, and a 120 km/h rally ball at 1.0-1.3 s baseline to baseline. These too are derivations, not measurements.

TS4 models spin as a template-driven "drift" vector, not computed Magnus force, so the useful target is the size of the spin's effect, not rpm. *Derived:* a 3,000 rpm forehand at 30 m/s has S ≈ 0.35 and C_L ≈ 0.3. Its Magnus acceleration ½ρC_LAv²/m is about **11-12 m/s², roughly 1.2 g of extra downward pull**. A 1,000 rpm flat drive at 35 m/s gets about 0.45 g. So effective gravity for tour topspin should be about 2.2 g, which is what lets a ball clear the net by 0.8-1.0 m and still land in.

Bounce is where surfaces differ, and TS4 has **no surface logic in the stepper**. One ground plane, plus per-template curves for speed loss `c40(spin_a)`, outgoing elevation `c58(θ)`, spin decay `c4C` and sidespin yaw `c64`, must express everything. Cross gives **vertical COR e_y ≈ 0.75 on hard courts, 0.6-0.75 on grass and about 0.85 on clay** (clay bounces higher because the ball digs in). Sliding friction is **μ ≈ 0.5-0.6 on Wimbledon grass and up to about 0.9 on clay**. The ball slides through the whole bounce below about **16-20° incidence**, and topspin narrows that sliding range while backspin widens it ([Cross, Physics Today 2008](https://physicstoday.aip.org/quick-study/tennis-physics-anyone)). The classic full-roll result, 0.645 of horizontal speed retained, overstates grip: real balls "bite" but don't roll ([Cross BOUNCE](https://www.physics.usyd.edu.au/~cross/BOUNCE.htm)). *Derived with Cross's sliding model (Δv_x = μ(1+e_y)v_y) for a 25 m/s ball arriving at 15° with no spin:* hard keeps about **72% of speed and leaves at about 16°**. Grass keeps about **74% and leaves lower, at about 14°**. Clay grips, so cap it at the rolling limit: about **65-68% at about 20°**, slower and higher. The ITF Court Pace Rating bands are **≤29 slow, 30-34 medium-slow, 35-39 medium, 40-44 medium-fast, ≥45 fast** ([ITF](https://www.itftennis.com/en/about-us/tennis-tech/classified-surfaces/)). The CPR formula itself was not verified and should not be used until it is checked against the ITF booklet.

## Errors, not winners, decide two points in three

Real match statistics give the targets for simulated outcomes, and the most important one runs against arcade instinct. At the 2015 French and US Opens, **men's points ended 32.5% in winners, 45.8% in unforced errors and 21.7% in forced errors; women's ended 32.4%, 49.4% and 18.2%** ([Tennis Abstract](https://tennisabstract.com/blog/?p=1484)). The forced/unforced split depends on the scorer's judgement, but the overall shape holds: errors end about two-thirds of points. A TS4 retune in which winners dominate the ending mix has failed its realism goal however good it feels.

**Serve.** ATP "serve effectiveness" is **58% of first serves: 16% aces, 22% unreturned and 20% attacking first balls**. Second serves reach only 23% ([ATP Tour](https://www.atptour.com/en/news/insights-serve-effectiveness)). Elite men win about **69% of first-serve points on clay and 75% on grass and hard, and about 55% of second-serve points on any surface** ([match-analysis PDF](https://eclass-b.uoa.gr/modules/document/file.php/PHED739/Educational%20archives%20%28e-books%2C%20worksheets%2C%20pdfs%20etc%29/LInce%20plus%20software/Match%20analysis%20and%20probability%20of%20winning%20a%20point%20in%20elite%20men%E2%80%99s%20singles%20tennis.pdf)). Women at the 2019 Slams won 62.4% (clay) to 67.5% (hard) of first-serve points ([Prieto-Lage et al. 2022](https://rcastoragev2.blob.core.windows.net/0df265019bc0b82444ec87a604cdbb8c/PMC9266198.pdf)). The career ceiling is **Karlovic at 82.6%** of first-serve points won ([tennis-db](https://tennis-db.com/leaders/first-serve-points-won-pct/career?era=modern)), the natural cap for a maxed big-server build. Double faults run a little under **4% of service points** ([Tennis Abstract](https://www.tennisabstract.com/blog/category/serve-statistics/)). Hard-court ace rates are about **14-16% of service points** in 2026 ATP events ([Tennis Abstract](https://www.tennisabstract.com/cgi-bin/surface-speed.cgi?year=2026)). In the same WTA study, serves to the T or wide that ended in short rallies were won **64.9% (clay) to 86.3% (hard)**, and body serves 53.1-69.9%. Aiming wide already earns its reward in real tennis, which is why 2K25's "wide spreads more" rule is the right counterweight.

**Net play.** At Wimbledon, serve-and-volley fell from about **33% of men's serve points in 2002 to about 5% by 2017, yet it was won 65-70% of the time throughout** ([Tennishead](https://tennishead.net/?p=81659)). In charted elite grass matches, S&V share collapsed from **about 81% (1990s) to 7% (2010s)**, while net-point win rates *rose*, from 65.2% to 69.2%. Passers put the first pass in play **65-73%** of the time but hit clean passing winners only **about 21% in every era**. First-volley winners rose to 32% ([Tennis Abstract](https://www.tennisabstract.com/blog/?p=3584)). Across more than 84,000 approaches since 2020, about **96% of approach shots land in, the net player wins 58.6% when the approach comes back, and approaches through the middle win 70.8%** ([Tennis Abstract](https://www.tennisabstract.com/blog/category/research)). The key point is that **a 65-70% net win rate reflects selection, not the strength of the net position**. The game should reproduce it only when players come in off good balls. Approaching off neutral or defensive balls has to win well below 65%, or the net becomes a dominant strategy.

**Drop shots.** Across about 35,000 charted ATP drop shots, the **hitter wins 53.8%**. Drop shots appear on **4.6% of points**, about one-third end the point immediately, and about **19% are the hitter's own unforced errors**. Specialists reach 62-70% (Alcaraz 62.1%, Nishikori 69.6%) ([Tennis Abstract](https://www.tennisabstract.com/blog/2024/03/21/effects-and-after-effects-of-the-carlos-alcaraz-drop-shot/)). Forehand drops win about 51.5% and backhand drops only about 39.7%, and tour-wide forehand success *fell* from 52.4% to 48.2% as more players used it ([ATP Tour](https://www.atptour.com/en/news/forehand-drop-shot-success-alcaraz)). That last figure is an adaptation effect worth reproducing: the drop shot is a near-coin-flip option whose value depends on surprise, not a winner button. No public data exists on retrieval odds by defender distance, or on putaway and overhead conversion. The short-ball targets below are therefore proposals anchored only to the approach and net figures.

**Rally length and movement.** At the 2019 WTA Slams, **63.7-70.9% of points ended within 0-4 shots**. Clay produced more 5-8 and 9+ rallies, and servers won the short rallies while medium and long ones split close to 50/50 ([Prieto-Lage et al. 2022](https://rcastoragev2.blob.core.windows.net/0df265019bc0b82444ec87a604cdbb8c/PMC9266198.pdf)). On grass, Alcaraz's 2023 Queen's run split **75/20/5** ([ATP Infosys](https://www.atptour.com/en/news/alcaraz-infosys-atp-beyond-the-numbers-june-2024)). Players run about **9.6 m per point (men) and 8.2 m (women)** ([PeerJ 2024](https://peerj.com/articles/18263.pdf)), and almost never above **16 mph (about 26 km/h)** ([BBC Bitesize mirror](https://bbclatestnews.pages.dev/bitesize/articles/zn9y6v4)). Expert returners land the split step **about 133 ms after the server's contact** and anticipate before contact on fewer than 10% of returns ([Sport-IAT record](https://lida.sport-iat.de/ta/Record/4074436)). Collegiate players land at about 180 ms ([Sponet](https://sponet.de/sponet/Record/4042098)), and a 200 km/h serve leaves about 500-600 ms to respond ([Mecheri et al.](https://pure.port.ac.uk/ws/files/13666713/Mecheri_et_al._Accepted.pdf)). About 80% of returns land in at Slam level (same source). The movement implication is stark. TS4's reach scorer rejects required speeds above **50 m/s**, while real elite top speed is about **7 m/s**. The 50 m/s cut is only a sanity bound, so realism has to come from the Rush/Jog speed tables and the reaction delay.

## Risk must be opt-in, legible and always countered

The design literature turns these targets into rules. Start with error. Sports games build accuracy in layers: a stat-based base dispersion, multiplied by situation (balance, contest, incoming difficulty, fatigue) and then by timing. NBA 2K16's director said a green release **roughly doubles make probability** (40% to 80%) and that shooters under about 30% get no green frames ([Complex](https://www.complex.com/sports/2016/02/perfect-2k16-jumper)). Two cautionary tales define the edges. A FIFA 19 bug made a mistimed finesse shot *more* accurate than an untimed one, which is an exploit by definition ([Kotaku](https://kotaku.com/fifa-19-s-latest-update-fixes-overpowered-goal-shooting-1831955831)). EA FC 26 then removed timed finishing entirely, because the timing bonus disproportionately rewarded long-range shots ([Insider Gaming](https://insider-gaming.com/is-timed-finishing-in-ea-fc-26/)). MLB The Show shrinks the contact zone on tough pitches. That is the closest analogue to "a hard incoming ball shrinks your window", and players resent it because it is invisible ([Operation Sports](https://www.operationsports.com/why-pci-shrinkage-isnt-changing-in-mlb-the-show-26/)). For TS4 this means three things. The Good timing grade should improve dispersion by at most about 2x. A Bad grade must always be worse than a neutral tap or control shot. Incoming-ball difficulty should shrink the window in a way the player can see.

The risk model should use an aim margin, not a scalar "error chance". With Gaussian landing error, aiming **1σ inside a line faults about 16% of the time on that side, 2σ about 2.3%, and 0.5σ about 31%**. These are standard normal tail values that a tuner can target per shot class. Power should mostly add long and net error, aggressive angles should add wide error, and heavy topspin should cut long error at a cost in pace. TS4 has a structural constraint here. On the first bounce the ball **snaps to the planned landing point P1**, and normal rally shots run with the net-crossing check off, so netted and long balls are evidently chosen as an *outcome mode* (Net, Out, Wood, LetIn/LetOut) before the trajectory is built. That makes dispersion a pre-launch decision: sample the landing point and a net-clearance error, compare them with the lines and the net, pick the mode, and only then solve the flight. The code that picks the mode has not been located yet and is the single most important thing to find.

On builds, Sirlin's test is that a game is balanced when many options are viable at high level, and **the first job is to empty the top tier**, not to make everything identical ([Sirlin Part 1](https://www.sirlin.net/articles/balancing-multiplayer-games-part-1-definitions)). Schreiber and Romero frame an overpowered option as an *undercosted* one ([Game Balance](https://www.routledge.com/9781498799577)). TopSpin 2K25 lets a capped player max only about 4 of 8 attributes ([Charlie Intel](https://charlieintel.com/games/topspin-2k25-best-coaches-myplayer-attributes-to-level-up-321533)), yet guides still say to raise Power and Backhand first whatever the style ([Yardbarker](https://www.yardbarker.com/tennis/articles/topspin_2k25_attributes_guide_the_best_stats_to_upgrade/s1_17124_40315495)). That is evidence that budgets alone don't stop min-maxing when marginal value per point is unequal. NBA 2K ties attribute caps to body dimensions and limits cap breakers to +5 per attribute ([2K](https://nba.2k.com/2k26/courtside-report/myplayer-builder/)). TS4 already has a bundling mechanism in its style tracks. What it lacks is evidence of saturating output curves and of parity in marginal value across tracks and coaches.

Counterplay comes from double-blind commitment. In Sirlin's Yomi model, every strong move needs a counter about three layers deep, and **simultaneous commitment makes weaker options viable** ([Sirlin Part 2](https://sirlin.net/articles/balancing-multiplayer-games-part-2-viable-options)). For tennis, the defender commits to an anticipation lean near the attacker's contact. A correct read buys ticks of reaction and a wrong one costs ticks, and the attacker can disguise direction by locking aim late. This is the mechanism behind "a perfect read still gives a slim chance": the attacker's odds stay high on average, but every outcome remains contestable. Real returners anticipate before contact on fewer than 10% of returns, so the read should be a gamble, not a habit. Shot relationships should come from geometry and court state, not hard-coded "beats" rules. Depth beats the drop, the drop beats the deep baseliner, the approach beats the short ball, the pass and lob beat the net rusher, and heavy deep topspin beats the approach. For acceptance testing, Riot's framework flags a champion when its win rate exceeds **54.5% at a low ban rate, falling to 52.5% as ban rate rises** ([Riot](https://nexus.leagueoflegends.com/en-au/2019/05/dev-champion-balance-framework/)). Zook, Harrison and Riedl use agents of varying skill to expose options that dominate only at low or only at high skill ([arXiv 1908.01423](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1908.01423)).

Feel is separate from power. Hit-stop, sound coherence and camera control drive perceived impact, and too much juice cheapens it ([Lin et al. 2022](https://faculty.washington.edu/zkwen/articles/lin22features.pdf)). 2K25's perfect bonus is deliberately only "a slight uptick in angle and power" ([Tom's Guide](https://www.tomsguide.com/gaming/topspin-2k25-review)). A serve-and-volley point should feel great because the serve *earned* a weak return that sits up, and because the volley lands with tiered audiovisual feedback, not because the volley button is strong. Showing why a shot missed (frame-late, long by X cm) answers TS4's most repeated critique, nebulous timing, without reducing depth.

## Conclusion

Most of the retune is data work. The one structural change is to make shot outcomes depend on the setup that produced them. TS4's engine already decides outcomes before it builds the flight, and all of its physics is template data. That is an advantage: a single pre-launch outcome function that combines aim margin, power bucket, timing grade, incoming difficulty and fatigue can enforce "every shot has a purpose" more directly than a physics change could. The linear-drag finding is the one place where tuning against real numbers will fight the model. Matching both serve speed and lob hang time requires per-template drag, and any template shared across very different speeds will need to be split.

The real-world statistics carry the main design lesson. Strong tennis options win through selection, not raw strength. Net play wins 65-70% and drop shots 54% only because players choose when to use them. Hard balance goals therefore belong on *conditional* outcomes (approach off a short ball versus a neutral one; a drop from inside the baseline versus from behind it), not on how strong a shot is in isolation. A retune that hits the match-level targets while those conditional gaps stay wide will be both realistic and hard to min-max. Hitting the aggregate targets with narrow conditional gaps would mean the game has reproduced tennis's statistics without its decisions.
